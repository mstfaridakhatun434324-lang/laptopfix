If you join a lot of video meetings on a Windows 11 laptop, you have probably noticed that captions can come from two places at once: the system-wide Windows Live Captions that floats over everything, and the captions built into Microsoft Teams itself. They look similar, but they are built on entirely different technology — and choosing the wrong one can mean worse accuracy, higher battery drain, or captions that vanish the moment your connection hiccups.

The confusion is understandable. Both show real-time text, both support multiple languages, and both can run on a Copilot+ PC. But Teams captions are a cloud service tied to your meeting, while Live Captions is an operating-system feature that transcribes whatever audio your PC plays. Understanding that distinction answers nearly every "which should I use" question.

This guide compares the two head-to-head across accuracy, languages, privacy, offline behavior, and performance, so you can pick the right tool for each situation. ## Why this happens

- **Two separate caption engines:** Live Captions is a Windows OS feature using on-device or local speech models; Teams captions are generated by Microsoft's cloud speech service tied to the meeting session.
- **Different audio sources:** Teams captions transcribe the meeting's mixed audio stream from the cloud; Live Captions captures your PC's local output, including system sounds and other apps.
- **Speaker attribution gap:** Teams knows who is speaking and labels captions with names; Live Captions has no concept of meeting participants.
- **Language pipeline differences:** Teams supports real-time translation between languages in meetings; Live Captions translates too but with a different, device-side model set.
- **Offline capability split:** Teams captions die without internet; Live Captions on Copilot+ PCs keeps working fully offline using the NPU.
- **Performance footprint contrast:** Teams captions cost almost no local compute but require the heavy Teams app; Live Captions is light on Copilot+ NPUs but can tax CPU on older machines.


## How to fix it

Work through these in order — diagnose first, then act.
### Fix 1: Use Teams captions inside meetings for best accuracy

For actual meetings, Teams' built-in captions are the better default. They are tuned for meeting audio, identify speakers by name, and handle crosstalk and room echo better than a generic system transcriber.

1. Join the Teams meeting and open the meeting controls.
2. Click More (three dots), then Language and speech.
3. Turn on Live captions and set your spoken language.
4. Optionally enable translated captions for another language.

### Fix 2: Use Live Captions for everything outside Teams

Live Captions shines as a universal tool: webinars in a browser, recorded videos, podcasts, system audio, and any app without built-in captions. Turn it on with Win + Ctrl + L and it floats above all windows, transcribing whatever plays.

1. Press Win + Ctrl + L to launch Live Captions anywhere in Windows.
2. Open Settings > Accessibility > Live captions to customize style.
3. Enable the microphone option to also caption real-world speech.
4. Turn on translation if you need another language.

### Fix 3: Solve the duplicate-captions problem

Running both at once is the most common annoyance: two caption bars, slightly different text, twice the resource use. Decide based on context — Teams captions win inside Teams meetings, Live Captions wins everywhere else.

1. Before a meeting: enable Teams captions, disable Live Captions.
2. After the meeting: re-enable Live Captions if still needed.
3. Dock the Live Captions window at the top to reduce overlap.
4. Accept minor text differences — the pipelines differ by design.

### Fix 4: Fix Teams captions when they fail or lag

If Teams captions refuse to turn on, show perpetual "getting captions ready," or lag badly, the issue is almost always network or policy. First check your connection — Teams captions need a stable link to Microsoft's cloud; on flaky Wi-Fi they degrade or stop.

1. Verify your internet connection is stable during the meeting.
2. Confirm with your IT admin that transcription is allowed by policy.
3. Leave and rejoin the meeting to reset the caption stream.
4. Update the Teams desktop app to the latest version.

### Fix 5: Fix Live Captions when it underperforms

If Live Captions is the one misbehaving — blank window, wrong language, high resource use — work through its own checklist. Confirm the correct audio output is set as default in Sound settings, since Live Captions follows the default device. For official update guidance and troubleshooting steps, see the [Microsoft Windows support hub](https://support.microsoft.com/en-us/windows).

1. Set the correct default audio output in Sound settings.
2. Verify caption language settings under Accessibility > Live captions.
3. Update NPU drivers on Copilot+ PCs via your maker's support site.
4. Toggle Live Captions off and on to reattach the audio tap.

### Fix 6: Choose based on privacy and offline needs

Your situation may decide for you. If you handle sensitive meetings, know that Teams captions are processed in Microsoft's cloud — subject to your organization's data policies — while Live Captions on a Copilot+ PC can process entirely on-device, keeping audio off the network.

1. For sensitive content, prefer on-device Live Captions on Copilot+ PCs.
2. For offline use, Live Captions is the only working option.
3. For speaker names and saved transcripts, use Teams captions.
4. For real-time meeting translation, Teams captions are tuned better.

## Closing

Teams captions and Windows Live Captions are complementary, not competitors. Use Teams captions inside meetings for speaker names, accuracy, and transcripts; use Live Captions everywhere else for universal, on-device transcription that works offline.

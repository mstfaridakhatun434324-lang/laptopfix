A trackpad that suddenly sits crooked. A bottom panel that rocks on a flat table. A keyboard deck that flexes when you type. These are the quiet early signs of a swelling laptop battery — lithium cells puffing up with gas as their chemistry breaks down. It is the one battery problem where the right move is to stop troubleshooting and start protecting yourself.

This guide is not about reviving the battery. A swollen battery cannot be fixed, only replaced. What follows is the safe sequence: confirm it, secure your data, stop the risk from growing, and get rid of the battery properly.

## Why this happens

- **Natural chemical aging:** all lithium batteries slowly generate gas as their electrolyte breaks down; heat and age accelerate it.
- **Chronic heat exposure:** laptops that run hot for years — blocked vents, heavy gaming on a bed — age their batteries far faster.
- **Overcharging habits:** keeping a lithium battery pinned at 100 percent in a hot machine for months stresses the cells.
- **Manufacturing defects:** a small number of cells develop internal faults that produce gas early in their life.
- **Physical damage:** drops and dents can damage the separator inside a cell, triggering swelling later.
- **Using the wrong charger:** a charger delivering the wrong voltage can overstress the charging circuitry.
- **Deep discharges left uncharged:** a battery left fully dead for weeks can become unstable when finally recharged.

## How to fix it

Work through these in order. The goal is safety first, data second, replacement third.

### Fix 1: Confirm the swelling without pressing on it

1. Power the laptop off and unplug everything.
2. Set it on a flat, hard table and check for rocking or a visible bulge in the bottom panel.
3. Look at the trackpad and keyboard from the side — a raised or tilted trackpad is a classic tell.
4. Do not press, squeeze, or try to flatten anything. Just observe.

### Fix 2: Stop charging immediately

1. Unplug the charger right away if it is connected.
2. Do not plug it back in. Charging a swollen battery adds heat and energy to an already unstable cell.
3. Move the laptop to a cool, ventilated spot away from anything flammable — a tile floor or stone countertop is ideal. Never put it on a bed or sofa.

### Fix 3: Back up your data now

1. If the laptop still powers on, boot it on AC power only and copy your important files to an external drive or cloud storage immediately.
2. Do this in one session — do not leave the machine running for hours.
3. If the machine will not boot or the swelling is severe (cracked case, hissing, smell), skip this step entirely. No file is worth the risk.

### Fix 4: Power it down and leave it alone

1. Shut the laptop down fully once your backup is done.
2. Do not store it in a bag, drawer, or car. Leave it on a hard, non-flammable surface at room temperature.
3. Keep it away from children and pets until it is removed.

### Fix 5: Do not remove the battery yourself unless it is designed to come out

1. If your laptop has an externally removable battery (a latch on the bottom), you may slide it out gently and set it aside in a fire-safe spot.
2. If the battery is internal, do not pry the laptop open. Swollen pouches tear easily, and a puncture can cause a fire.
3. Take the whole laptop to the manufacturer's service center or a reputable repair shop and let them handle the removal.

### Fix 6: Arrange a proper replacement

1. Check whether the battery is under warranty — many manufacturers cover swelling as a defect. Start at [Dell support](https://www.dell.com/support), [HP support](https://support.hp.com/us-en), or [Lenovo support](https://support.lenovo.com/us/en/) for your brand.
2. Order a genuine replacement battery for your exact model number. Cheap third-party batteries are a common cause of repeat swelling.
3. Have it installed by the service center, or follow the manufacturer's service manual carefully if you are experienced.

### Fix 7: Dispose of the old battery correctly

1. Never throw a swollen battery in the trash or a regular recycling bin — it is a fire hazard for waste facilities.
2. Take it to a dedicated battery recycling drop-off, an electronics retailer with battery collection, or a manufacturer take-back program.
3. Transport it in a metal or ceramic container, terminals taped, away from heat and direct sun.

### Fix 8: Know the emergency signs

Most swelling is slow and undramatic, but know when it becomes urgent.

1. If you hear hissing, smell a sweet chemical odor, or see smoke, do not touch the laptop — move away and ventilate the room.
2. If it is safe to do so, unplug the charger from the wall (not from the laptop) to remove the power source.
3. A small lithium fire cannot be put out with water effectively; a Class D extinguisher or dry sand works, but your priority is getting people out and calling emergency services, not saving the laptop.
4. After any such incident, do not keep the battery in your home — hand it to a hazardous waste facility.

## Closing

A slightly swollen battery feels like a small problem, but it is the one battery issue you should never try to power through. Confirm it, back up, stop charging, and let a professional swap the pack. The laptop will be fine with a new battery — and you will have handled the one battery fault that can genuinely hurt someone. When in doubt, treat it as serious; caution costs nothing here. Share this guide with anyone who notices their trackpad sitting crooked — most people have never heard of battery swelling.

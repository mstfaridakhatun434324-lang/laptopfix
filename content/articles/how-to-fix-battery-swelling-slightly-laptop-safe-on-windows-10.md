If your Windows 10 laptop has started rocking on the desk like a wobbly table, or the bottom cover seam is lifting just enough to catch your fingernail, you're probably looking at a swelling battery. It's the hardware problem that deserves respect even when it looks small — because "slightly swollen" is the early stage of something that only gets worse.

Here's the practical version: what it means, how to stay safe this week, and how to get it fixed without drama. Nothing here requires opening the laptop yourself — every step is something you can do at your desk in a few minutes.

## Why this happens

- **Electrolyte breaking down into gas.** Heat and age decompose the liquid inside lithium cells; the gas has nowhere to go, so the pouch expands.
- **Living at 100% on the charger.** Windows 10 laptops without a charge limiter sit at full voltage for months, which accelerates gas buildup more than any other habit.
- **Hot running conditions.** Blocked vents, heavy workloads, and warm rooms all raise cell temperature and speed the process.
- **Repeated deep discharges.** Running to empty over and over stresses the chemistry.
- **Defective cells.** Some packs swell due to manufacturing flaws regardless of treatment.
- **Simple age.** After several years, minor swelling can appear in batteries that were treated reasonably well.
- **Damaged charging circuitry.** Uncommon, but a fault that overcharges will puff a battery fast.

## How to fix it

Swelling can't be fixed with settings. This is a safety-and-replacement playbook.

### Fix 1: Assess how bad it is

1. Shut down and unplug the laptop.
2. Set it on a known-flat table. Rock it gently — any wobble that wasn't there before is the battery pushing the bottom cover out.
3. Run your finger along the bottom seams and the keyboard deck edges, feeling for lifting or gaps.
4. Press the trackpad: if it feels raised or its click is mushy compared to before, the battery is pressing up from underneath.
5. Sniff near the vents. A sweet or solvent-like chemical smell means venting has started — skip to Fix 3's stop-use instructions immediately.

### Fix 2: Secure your files today

You may be without this laptop for days during repair — get your data safe now.

1. Plug in an external drive.
2. Open **Control Panel > System and Security > Backup and Restore (Windows 7)** and choose **Create a system image**, or simply copy your user folders manually.
3. If you use OneDrive, confirm important folders show the green synced checkmark, not a pending sync icon.
4. Open a couple of backed-up files to confirm they're intact.

### Fix 3: Safe-use rules until replacement

1. Charge only while you're in the room and awake. No overnight charging, no charging while you're out.
2. Use the laptop on a hard, flat, ventilated surface. Heat is the enemy — keep vents clear and skip gaming or video rendering.
3. Don't stack anything on the closed lid; pressure on a swollen pack is exactly what you want to avoid.
4. Stop using it entirely — powered off, unplugged, on a non-flammable surface — if you notice: growing swelling, significant heat, any smell, or visible case separation. These are "do not negotiate" signs.

### Fix 4: Check warranty, recall, and support options

1. Get your serial number from **Settings > System > About** or the bottom sticker.
2. Check your manufacturer's support site for warranty status and search for battery recalls on your model — [Dell](https://www.dell.com/support), [HP](https://support.hp.com/us-en), and [Lenovo](https://support.lenovo.com/us/en/) all publish recall lists.
3. A recall or active warranty usually means a free battery replacement through their process — follow it rather than opening the machine.
4. Out of warranty? Get quotes from the manufacturer service and one reputable local shop; compare before committing.

### Fix 5: Replace through the right channel

1. Confirm the exact battery part number for your model — never guess from "looks similar."
2. Buy genuine. Counterfeit batteries are the one place where saving money can literally backfire; poor cells swell sooner.
3. If your model has an externally removable battery, DIY is straightforward. If it's internal (most Windows 10-era ultrabooks), have a technician do it — prying near a swollen pack is not a beginner job.
4. When you hand the laptop over, tell the technician the battery is swollen. Repair shops handle these routinely, but they appreciate the heads-up so they can take the right precautions when opening the chassis.

### Fix 6: Handle the old battery like the hazard it is

1. Put electrical tape over the terminals to prevent shorts.
2. Store it somewhere cool and dry, away from metal objects, until you can drop it off.
3. Take it to a battery recycling point — electronics retailers and municipal hazardous-waste sites accept them.
4. Never bin it. Swollen lithium packs in garbage trucks are a genuine fire cause.

### Fix 7: Set up the new battery for a longer life

1. If your vendor's Windows 10 utility offers a charge threshold (Lenovo Vantage, Dell Power Manager, ASUS Battery Health Charging), cap at 80% if you live on AC power.
2. Keep the machine cool: clean vents yearly, use it on hard surfaces.
3. Avoid both extremes — don't store it at 0% for weeks, don't pin it at 100% for months.
4. Every few months, run `powercfg /batteryreport` in an elevated Command Prompt and glance at the wear trend. Our [battery health check guide](/articles/how-to-check-battery-health-on-windows-11-laptop/) explains the numbers, and if percentages start [jumping erratically](/articles/how-to-fix-battery-percentage-jumps-up-and-down-on-dell-laptop/), calibrate before assuming the worst.

## Closing

"Slightly" swollen is early — and early is good, because it means you have time to back up, check warranty, and replace the battery calmly. What you don't have is permission to ignore it: swelling never reverses, it only progresses. Act this week and it's a routine, affordable repair handled in a single visit..

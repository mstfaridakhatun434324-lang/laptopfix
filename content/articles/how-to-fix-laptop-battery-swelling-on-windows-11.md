Let's be direct: a swelling battery is not a troubleshooting problem. It's a safety problem. If your laptop's bottom panel is bulging, the trackpad is lifting, or the keyboard feels raised in the middle, the lithium-ion cells inside are producing gas — and a punctured swollen cell can vent, smoke, or catch fire. There is no Windows 11 setting that fixes this. What follows is what to do right now, and how to handle the replacement safely.

It helps to understand that swelling is a one-way process. The gas forms because the liquid electrolyte inside the cells is breaking down, and once that breakdown starts it doesn't stop or reverse — it only continues. That's why every step below is about safe removal and replacement, not repair. Anyone suggesting a software fix, a "battery reset," or a calibration for a swollen battery is giving dangerous advice.

## Why this happens

- **Gas buildup from electrolyte breakdown.** As lithium-ion cells age or overheat, the electrolyte decomposes and releases gas. The sealed pouch has nowhere to vent, so it inflates.
- **Chronic overcharging.** Keeping a laptop at 100% on the charger for months or years stresses the cells and accelerates gas formation.
- **Heat.** Laptops that run hot — gaming, video rendering, or just poor ventilation on a bed or couch — age their batteries dramatically faster.
- **Deep discharges.** Repeatedly draining to 0% damages cell chemistry and can trigger swelling in already-weakened cells.
- **Manufacturing defects.** A small percentage of batteries swell due to internal flaws regardless of how well they're treated. This is why swelling can appear on relatively new machines.
- **Physical damage.** A drop or puncture can damage the separator inside a cell, starting a slow swelling process that appears weeks later.
- **Age.** Even a well-treated battery can swell after 4–6 years as the chemistry naturally degrades.

## How to fix it

Work through these in order. Steps 1–3 are immediate safety actions; the rest cover replacement.

### Fix 1: Stop charging it — right now

1. Unplug the charger immediately. Do not plug it back in with the swollen battery installed.
2. Save your work quickly and shut the laptop down via **Start** > **Power** > **Shut down**.
3. Move the laptop to a hard, non-flammable surface away from paper, fabric, and direct sunlight.
4. Do not press on the swollen area, and do not try to "flatten" it. Pressure on a swollen pouch cell is exactly what causes ruptures.

### Fix 2: Assess how bad the swelling is

1. Look at the bottom panel, trackpad, and keyboard for bulging, gaps, or warping.
2. If the trackpad has popped up or the case seams are splitting, treat it as urgent — the battery comes out today, not next week.
3. If you smell a sweet chemical odor or see any hissing, move the laptop somewhere ventilated and don't handle it more than necessary. Venting batteries release irritating fumes.
4. Take a photo of the swelling from the side. It helps the repair shop (or the manufacturer's support team) assess the situation, and it documents the condition in case of a warranty claim — swollen batteries on machines under warranty are often replaced free.

### Fix 3: Back up your data before the repair

You'll be opening the laptop or handing it to a technician, so back up first.

1. With the battery still installed but the charger unplugged, power on briefly only if the swelling is mild — copy critical files to an external drive or cloud storage.
2. If swelling is severe, don't power it on at all; remove the drive or let the technician handle data after the battery is out.

### Fix 4: Remove the swollen battery safely

1. Power off and unplug everything.
2. Remove the bottom panel and locate the battery. Photograph the cable routing before touching anything.
3. Disconnect the battery cable from the motherboard first, then unscrew the battery.
4. Lift it out gently without bending, puncturing, or applying pressure. If it's stuck with adhesive, use plastic tools and patience — never metal prying tools near a swollen cell.
5. Place it in a fire-safe spot (a metal container or ceramic dish) away from flammables until disposal.

If you're not comfortable doing this, any reputable repair shop will remove a swollen battery for a small fee. This is genuinely worth paying for if the swelling is severe.

### Fix 5: Dispose of it properly — never in the trash

1. Take the swollen battery to a battery recycling drop-off: electronics retailers, municipal hazardous-waste collection, or a manufacturer take-back program.
2. Tape over the battery terminals with electrical tape before transport to prevent short circuits.
3. Never put a swollen lithium battery in household trash or regular recycling — it's a fire hazard for waste facilities.

### Fix 6: Install a genuine replacement

1. Order a genuine battery matched to your exact model and part number. Manufacturer support pages — [Dell](https://www.dell.com/support), [HP](https://support.hp.com/us-en), [Lenovo](https://support.lenovo.com/us/en/) — list the correct part numbers so you don't guess.
2. Avoid cheap third-party packs for a machine that already had a battery failure; poor quality control is a known swelling trigger.
3. After installation, generate a battery report with `powercfg /batteryreport` to confirm the new battery reports sane capacity numbers.

### Fix 7: Prevent it from happening again

1. Enable your manufacturer's charge limit (Lenovo Conservation Mode, ASUS Battery Health Charging, Dell charge thresholds) to cap charging at 60–80% when you mostly work plugged in. See [how to fix a Lenovo charge threshold](/articles/how-to-fix-battery-threshold-not-working-lenovo/) if yours won't stick.
2. Keep the laptop ventilated — hard surfaces, clean vents, no marathon sessions on a blanket.
3. Don't leave it at 100% on the charger for weeks at a time if you can avoid it.

## Closing

Swelling is the one battery symptom where speed matters more than diagnosis. Stop charging, get the battery out safely, dispose of it properly, and replace it with a genuine part. Everything else — charge limits, ventilation, avoiding permanent 100% — is about making sure you never see this again.
